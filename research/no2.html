Reactive nitrogen

Inferring stratospheric NO₂ photolysis rates from satellite observations

Can satellite composition measurements constrain stratospheric NO₂ photolysis without directly measuring sunlight? I develop a method using MIPAS observations and steady-state chemistry that agrees closely with modeled photolysis rates. It also captures enhanced photolysis over polar ice and snow, providing an observational test of the role of surface reflectivity in stratospheric chemistry.

Publication: Guan et al. (2023), ACP, 23(18), 10413–10422

Methods and contribution

Rapid daytime exchange between NO and NO₂ allows the photolysis rate to be inferred from simultaneous MIPAS measurements of atmospheric composition. I apply the method over southern high latitudes and compare the results with WACCM6 photolysis rates based on TUV radiative-transfer calculations, sampled at the observation times and locations.

This approach connects the radiation environment to chemical rates inferred from observations. Together with my organic aerosol photolysis work, it provides observational constraints on the chemical processes that shape stratospheric composition.
