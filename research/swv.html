Radiative forcing Stratospheric water vapor

Satellite-constrained stratospheric water vapor radiative forcing from methane oxidation

How much additional warming comes from stratospheric water vapor produced by methane oxidation? I combine satellite methane records with chemistry–climate models to constrain its radiative forcing to about 10% of methane's direct forcing. The agreement between observations and model ensembles points to radiative transfer as the dominant remaining uncertainty.

Publication: Guan & Solomon (2026), Geophysical Research Letters — under review

Methods and contribution

I combine the HALOE, MIPAS, and ACE-FTS methane records with CCMI-2022 and AerChemMIP model ensembles. An age-of-air framework compares local stratospheric methane with its abundance when the air entered the stratosphere, isolating methane oxidation and the associated water vapor production.

I translate the observational constraint into radiative forcing and apply it to future methane pathways. This connects changes in stratospheric composition to their climate effects, including an additional benefit of methane mitigation beyond reducing its direct warming.
