Stratospheric aerosols Model development

Limited long-term photolysis of stratospheric organic aerosols

How long does sunlight continue to destroy organic aerosols in the stratosphere? Aircraft and satellite observations indicate that photolysis becomes negligible as aerosols age. I use these constraints to develop an age-dependent photolysis scheme that improves CESM's representation of stratospheric organic aerosol abundance.

Publication: Guan et al. (2025), JAMES, 17(11), e2025MS005084

Methods and contribution

I compare aircraft aerosol composition measurements and OMPS satellite extinction profiles with CESM simulations of background aerosols and Australian wildfire smoke. The observations provide complementary tests of how long organic aerosol persists in the stratosphere.

The revised scheme uses air age as a proxy for aerosol aging and switches off photolysis after about 50 days, representing the decline in photolysis as aerosols undergo photobleaching. Together with my satellite-based NO₂ photolysis work, this project uses observations to constrain the chemical processes that control stratospheric composition.
